Hexagon Mesh

What can you do with the embeddings?

Four plain examples for a company with a large pile of public English text: search, SEO, product catalogs and research.

What you get back

For every text you send, you get a list of 384 numbers (a vector). Texts that mean similar things get vectors that sit close together. Everything below uses that one idea: measure how close two vectors are, then sort or group by it.

Phones do not replace data centers. Hexagon Mesh is a low-cost option for large, patient batches of public text. It computes the vectors. Storing them and searching them is your job, and a live search box needs answers in seconds, which this service is not built for.

Four ways to use it

Search by meaning

Embed: your articles, help pages, documentation or papers, split into pieces of up to 256 tokens.

Then: embed a visitor's question the same way and return the pieces whose vectors are closest.

Evidence: on the SciFact benchmark (300 questions, 5,183 science abstracts) the model scored 64.5 nDCG@10, matching its published score. measured on the real network

Watch for: you need a way to embed each new question quickly. The overnight batch is for the archive, not for live queries.

SEO: related pages and duplicates

Embed: the text of the pages on your own site.

Then: for each page, find the pages with the closest vectors and link them to each other, or flag pairs so close that they may say the same thing.

Watch for: English only, and 256 tokens per text, so long pages should be cut into sections. Whether this helps your rankings is for you to test; we have no measurement of it.

Product catalogs

Embed: public product titles and descriptions.

Then: suggest similar products, group near-duplicate listings, or let shoppers search by meaning instead of exact words.

Watch for: descriptions must be public and in English. Prices, stock and customer data do not belong in the text you send.

Research

Embed: abstracts, articles or other public text you want to study.

Then: group similar documents, find the nearest neighbours of one document, or look for gaps between groups.

Evidence: three phones embedded 3,225,386 arXiv abstracts in 18 hours 37 minutes. measured on the real network

Watch for: this is one small, general English model. Specialist vocabulary may be handled less well than by a model made for it.

How big a job is realistic?

Three phones embedded all of Simple English Wikipedia: 644,083 passages from 278,882 articles, 67.9 million tokens, with every result checked on a second phone. It was run twice. The first run, in October 2026, took 4 hours 27 minutes (shown on the dataset card). The latest run took 3 hours 18 minutes (shown on the home page). Both are measured on the real network.

The network is small and very large jobs have not been timed, so we do not promise a finish time for them. The dataset is public, so you can look at real output before you decide: Simple Wikipedia on phones.

The price is $0.005 per million tokens, and the first 1 billion tokens every month are free. Work out the cost of your own job.

When not to use it

  • Anything private or personal. Public, non-personal text only.
  • Languages other than English.
  • Answers needed in seconds.
  • A different model. Only all-MiniLM-L6-v2 is offered.
